## Key Facts

- **Client:** Taxd, a UK digital tax filing platform.
- **Commercial result:** 52 new paying customers through AI search over 12 months.
- **Supporting visibility:** Taxd appeared in 15 of 30 tracked Perplexity answers comparing Self Assessment filing tools.
- **Further evidence:** Taxd appeared in 14 of 30 tracked Perplexity answers about software for meeting Self Assessment deadlines.
- **Visibility reporting period:** 18 October–16 November 2026.

## Reaching people while they decide who to use

Taxd helps people complete their tax returns online, with support from tax specialists. Its customers include self-employed people, landlords and people living overseas who need to file a UK return.

Those customers can have very different needs. Someone filing for the first time may want a straightforward explanation of the process. Someone with a more complicated situation may be comparing software, checking which services support them and deciding how much help they need.

For Taxd, that creates an opportunity to appear while people are researching their options.

A potential customer does not have to know the Taxd name before an AI answer can introduce it. They can describe their circumstances or ask for a comparison of suitable services.

Our focus was on making Taxd easier to discover in those conversations, then helping visitors understand the service when they reached the website.

## Bringing search visibility and the website together

Our work with Taxd covered AEO, landing pages and copy alongside its wider search acquisition activity. That gave us a connected view of discovery and the experience a potential customer had after clicking through.

### Understanding the questions behind a purchase

Our tracking for Taxd includes questions about Self Assessment software, filing from overseas, rental income and other tax filing needs.

These questions help us examine where Taxd appears, which other providers are named and what information the answers draw on. Questions that do not include the Taxd name are particularly useful for assessing discovery: they show whether the platform introduces the business when asked about a relevant service.

The research also helps distinguish a broad request for information from a comparison that is closer to choosing a provider.

### Improving the information available about Taxd

The work included technical foundations, structured data, service pages and content that answered customer questions directly.

The aim was to make the business and its services easier to understand: who Taxd helps, what the platform does and what support a customer can expect. Clear explanations also give visitors useful information for deciding whether to take the next step.

This connected the AI search work with the landing pages and copy. A recommendation creates an opportunity; the website still needs to explain the offer clearly and help the visitor act.

### Checking the answers over time

Our tracking lets us review repeated answers to the tracked questions. We can see whether Taxd is included, how it is described and which pages are referenced.

That gives us evidence to review alongside the website work, rather than relying on a single favourable answer.

As Arjun Kumar, Taxd’s co-founder, puts it:

> “What we value most is his ability to provide clear insights, suggest improvements, and execute independently.”

## What the tracking data shows

Between **18 October and 16 November 2026**, Taxd appeared regularly in Perplexity’s monitored answers to two relevant software-selection questions.

| Tracked question category | Answers that mentioned Taxd | Visibility in those checks |
|---|---:|---:|
| Comparing Self Assessment filing tools | 15 of 30 | 50% |
| Software for meeting Self Assessment deadlines | 14 of 30 | 46.7% |

Neither tracked question included Taxd’s name.

That matters because the checks tested whether an AI answer would surface Taxd when asked about a service or a choice of software. They were not simply asking the platform to describe an already-named business.

These are results for two specific questions on Perplexity. They show relevant areas of visibility within the tracked set, rather than Taxd’s visibility across every tax-related AI search.

## Taxd’s own content appeared as a source

The answer text gives us another useful piece of evidence.

In a Perplexity response recorded on **15 November 2026**, Taxd was included among the filing options discussed. The answer also linked directly to Taxd’s [Self Assessment software comparison article](https://www.taxd.co.uk/blog/best-tax-software-for-uk-self-assessment-in-2026).

This is a concrete example of Taxd’s own content being referenced in an answer about choosing a service.

It illustrates the role that useful comparison content can play: explaining the available options, helping a reader understand the differences and giving the answer a relevant page to reference. It also makes the visibility tangible. We can inspect the recommendation and the link behind it.

## The commercial result: 52 new paying customers

Taxd recorded **52 new paying customers through AI search over a 12-month reporting period**.

That is the main result of this case study. It connects AI search with people choosing and paying for the service.

The tracking figures add context by showing where Taxd appeared in monitored answers and how its content was referenced. They measure a different part of the journey from customer acquisition, over a different reporting window.

For a business investing in AI search, both are useful. Visibility helps identify where potential customers could encounter the business. Customer reporting shows the commercial outcome attributed to that channel.
