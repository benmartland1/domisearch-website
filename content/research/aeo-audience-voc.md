# AEO Audience Research — Pain Points, Dream Outcomes & Voice/Tone

> **How to use this doc:** Sections 1–5 are for strategy (who we're writing for and what they actually feel). Section 6 is the vocabulary & tone playbook for the page itself. Section 7 is concrete copy direction — hooks, headlines, proof blocks and what to avoid. Quote attribution is given inline. Where a phrase is in **bold italics** without a citation, it's a composite/representative phrase distilled from the research — use it as copy inspiration, not as a real customer quote.

> **Note on sources:** live Reddit threads couldn't be scraped, so verbatim Reddit quotes are limited. The voice captured here comes from (a) named practitioner quotes in published articles (Robert Rose at CMI, Will Scott, HubSpot's Yamini Rangan, Ricardo McCoy), (b) industry reports from 2025–2026 that paraphrase community sentiment, and (c) distilled voice patterns that recur across multiple sources. Treat the real quotes as gold and the composite phrases as language starters to A/B test against real calls.

## 1. Who is actually buying AEO? — Four audience segments

Almost every article, podcast, and forum thread on AEO has at least one of these four people in the background. The page copy needs to let each of them self-identify in the first 5 seconds.

### Segment A — "The Panicked Founder / SMB Owner"

- **Context:** Solo founder, small services business, or SMB marketer. Watched organic traffic fall off a cliff in 2024–2025. Has heard ChatGPT/Perplexity mentioned in pitches and doesn't want to be left behind.
- **Emotional state:** Anxious, time-poor, allergic to jargon, skeptical of agencies but also terrified of inaction.
- **Level of knowledge:** Knows "AI is changing search." Doesn't know the difference between AEO, GEO, LLMO — and honestly doesn't care what it's called.
- **What they want from the page:** Plain-English explanation, proof this isn't another SEO agency in a new trench coat, a clear "what do I actually get" list, and price transparency (or at least a starting number).
- **Representative voice:** ***"I just want to know if my customers can find me when they ask ChatGPT. My traffic is down and I don't know what's real anymore."***

### Segment B — "The In-House SEO / Head of Growth"

- **Context:** Owns organic at a mid-market company. Has built a team and strategy around Google. Now has a CMO or CEO breathing down their neck about AI.
- **Emotional state:** Defensive, professionally insecure, quietly worried about career relevance. Has seen *a lot* of rebranded "next big thing" services and is allergic to hype.
- **Level of knowledge:** High. Reads Aleyda Solís, Kevin Indig, Lily Ray. Knows the terminology better than most agencies pitching them.
- **What they want:** Substance over buzzwords. Methodology. Someone who will say the thing 80% of AEO agencies are too polite to admit (***"most of this is just great SEO with better entity work"***) while proving the 20% that's genuinely new. No pretending AEO is a brand-new planet.
- **Representative voice (distilled from Will Scott, Search Engine Land, 2026):** *"GEO is just SEO if you've been doing good SEO. But the problem is so much SEO that gets done is still the same old thing."* ([Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/guide/seo-vs-geo-is-wrong))

### Segment C — "The SaaS Marketer Chasing LLM Citations"

- **Context:** Marketing lead at a B2B SaaS. Has just seen a board deck showing 47% of buyers prefer ChatGPT as their first research tool ([Position Digital, 2026](https://www.position.digital/blog/ai-seo-statistics/)) and that **"89% of B2B buyers already use AI to research products and services."** Watching competitors get named in answers while their own brand is invisible.
- **Emotional state:** Urgent. Opportunity-driven rather than panic-driven. Wants to win this window.
- **Level of knowledge:** Medium-high. Understands share-of-voice, prompt coverage, citation rate. Will absolutely ask about measurement.
- **What they want:** Strategy that ladders to pipeline (not vanity citations). Case studies in comparable ICPs (especially SaaS). A measurement framework they can show their CRO.
- **Representative voice:** ***"Every AI response that mentions [competitor] and not us is a deal we're already losing before the buyer hits our site."*** (paraphrased from multiple AI-visibility tool landing pages, April 2026)

### Segment D — "The Agency / Consultant Looking for a Partner or Reseller"

- **Context:** Runs a marketing agency that wants to add AEO without hiring specialists. Or an in-house team that wants to outsource the hard bits.
- **Emotional state:** Pragmatic. Looking for a credible specialist, not a competitor.
- **Level of knowledge:** High on client-management, patchy on LLM internals.
- **What they want:** Methodology transparency, white-label potential, confidence you won't embarrass them in front of their client.
- **Representative voice:** ***"I need a specialist I can put in a room with my client without having to translate."***

> **Copy implication:** The page should stratify. A confident plain-English hero for Segments A/C, with a "for SEOs / agencies" scroll-deep section that uses insider vocabulary and shows methodology (for B/D). Don't pick one and alienate the other three.

## 2. Pain points — what's actually bothering them

Ordered by how often the pain showed up across sources, not by which is most "marketable."

### 2.1 Traffic collapse is real and measurable

The fear isn't theoretical. Published 2025–2026 numbers this audience is quoting at each other:

- **Organic CTR dropped 61%** on queries where AI Overviews appear (from 1.76% to 0.61%) ([Dataslayer, 2026](https://www.dataslayer.ai/blog/google-ai-overviews-the-end-of-traditional-ctr-and-how-to-adapt-in-2025))
- **AI Overviews reduce clicks by 58%** across affected queries ([Ahrefs, via Search Atlas 2025](https://searchatlas.com/news/reddit-seo-data/))
- **60% of searches end without a click; 77% on mobile** ([Digital Bloom, 2025](https://thedigitalbloom.com/learn/2025-organic-traffic-crisis-analysis-report/))
- **HubSpot: traffic down 70–80%. Forbes: down 50%. CNN: down 27–38%** ([Digital Bloom, 2025](https://thedigitalbloom.com/learn/2025-organic-traffic-crisis-analysis-report/))
- **33% drop in Google referrals to news publishers globally** (12 months to Nov 2025)

Language this audience uses for it:
- **"Zero-click era"**
- **"The Great Decoupling"** (more searches, fewer clicks)
- **"Traffic apocalypse"** / **"Organic traffic crisis"**
- **"Getting absolutely cooked by AI Overviews"** (reddit tone)
- **"My organic is bleeding out"**

**Real quote, HubSpot CEO Yamini Rangan:** *"Organic search traffic is declining globally and AI overviews are giving answers, and fewer people are clicking through to websites."* ([Digital Bloom, 2025](https://thedigitalbloom.com/learn/2025-organic-traffic-crisis-analysis-report/))

**Real quote, industry sentiment on HubSpot's collapse:** *"If HubSpot, with one of the best SEO teams in the world, can experience this, none of us are safe."* ([Digital Bloom, 2025](https://thedigitalbloom.com/learn/2025-organic-traffic-crisis-analysis-report/))

> **Copy hook:** lead with the traffic-loss reality, specifically naming brands they respect (HubSpot, Forbes) so they feel *"if it's happening to them…"*. Don't waste time convincing them the problem exists — they already know.

### 2.2 Invisibility in AI answers while competitors get named

This is the pain that converts. They can see it happening in real time:

- They ask ChatGPT *"best [category] for [use case]"* and a competitor comes up.
- Their own customers message them saying *"ChatGPT recommended [competitor] — why aren't you on that list?"*
- **"If you're not being included in AI's answers, you're invisible."** (composite, recurs in dozens of agency blogs)

**Real quote, Ricardo McCoy (McCoy Marketing Services):** *"Focus on being problem solvers by answering real questions sharing insights, and speaking in a way that actually connects."* ([CMSWire, 2026](https://www.cmswire.com/digital-marketing/reddits-rise-in-ai-citations-what-marketers-must-know-about-aeo-strategy/))

Language:
- **"We're not in the answer"**
- **"Invisible to ChatGPT"**
- **"My competitor keeps getting cited"**
- **"I want to be the brand AI recommends"**
- **"ChatGPT isn't mentioning us"**

### 2.3 Measurement is a nightmare

This is the pain point that makes them distrust AEO providers:

- **"73% of brands running AEO campaigns in 2025 had no way to connect AI citations to actual revenue. They were optimizing blind."** ([SEM Nexus, 2026](https://semnexus.com/is-aeo-worth-it-the-true-roi-of-answer-engine-optimization-in-2026/))
- **"You'll never get a notification that says 'your Reddit thread was cited by ChatGPT.'"** ([Adexpert, 2026](https://adexpert.io/is-reddit-dead-for-seo-and-chatgpt/))
- LLMs are **probabilistic, not deterministic** — you can ask the same question minutes apart and get different results.

**Real quote, Robert Rose (Content Marketing Institute):** *"Your brand might be cited in an answer today. Tomorrow, it might vanish entirely. And there's absolutely nothing you can do about that."* ([CMI](https://contentmarketinginstitute.com/seo-for-content/answer-engine-optimization))

Pain sub-points: no GSC equivalent, volatility between runs, no unified attribution from ChatGPT/Perplexity/Gemini/AIO to revenue.

### 2.4 The "is this legit or snake oil" anxiety

This is EVERYWHERE. Every buyer has been burned by SEO agencies, and they're primed to assume AEO is the next round. You *must* address this on the page — directly.

- **John Mueller (Google) has said the acronym "signals scams."** ([Helloroketto, 2026](https://www.helloroketto.com/articles/aio-aeo-geo-aiso-llmo))
- **"Reddit called AEO/GEO 'bullshit buzzwords.'"** ([Helloroketto, 2026](https://www.helloroketto.com/articles/aio-aeo-geo-aiso-llmo))
- Widely shared: *"Agencies in the budget tier are simply traditional SEO mills that have slapped the letters 'AEO' onto their pricing page."* ([SEM Nexus, 2026](https://semnexus.com/is-aeo-worth-it-the-true-roi-of-answer-engine-optimization-in-2026/))
- Common accusation: *"Rebranding SEO at 2× the price."*

**Real quote, Robert Rose (CMI):** *"Every day, a new hyped-up post tells you that you should optimize all your content for generative AI search."* ([CMI](https://contentmarketinginstitute.com/seo-for-content/answer-engine-optimization))

**Real quote, Robert Rose (CMI):** *"The rallying cry goes: 'You've got to be the answer! Your brand will be right there in the AI result!' Which sounds great — until you ask, 'Then what?'"* ([CMI](https://contentmarketinginstitute.com/seo-for-content/answer-engine-optimization))

**Real quote, Robert Rose (CMI):** *"That's not a strategy. That's just shrugging loudly."* ([CMI](https://contentmarketinginstitute.com/seo-for-content/answer-engine-optimization))

> **Copy implication:** don't pretend the skepticism isn't there. A short section called something like *"Yes, a lot of AEO is rebadged SEO. Here's what's actually different about ours."* will do more work than any hero headline.

### 2.5 The internal-buy-in pain

Particularly for Segments B & C: they need to justify budget to a CFO or CMO who only understands "traffic."

- "How do I explain this to my CEO?"
- "I can't get budget for something I can't measure."
- "My board asked why we're not in ChatGPT. I didn't have an answer."

**Real quote (industry-wide):** *"Marketing teams can struggle to get internal buy-in to practice answer engine optimization."* ([CXL, 2026](https://cxl.com/blog/answer-engine-optimization-aeo-the-comprehensive-guide/))

> **Copy implication:** offer a "what to tell your board" section or a downloadable one-pager. It's uniquely useful to this buyer and most competitors don't do it.

### 2.6 Career/relevance anxiety (Segment B)

Quieter but powerful. SEOs who've built a decade of Google expertise now asking if it matters. Don't mock this or exploit it — respect it. This is the segment most likely to hire you if you show them *you can make them look smart in front of their org*, not replace them.

## 3. Dream outcomes — what "winning" looks like to them

When pushed to describe success, this is what they name (and the language they use):

### The concrete / measurable dreams

- **"Getting cited"** — the #1 phrase. *"I want to be cited when someone asks ChatGPT about [category]."*
- **"Share of voice in LLMs"** — *"Beat [competitor] in ChatGPT."*
- **"AI-referred traffic that converts"** — backed by: *"LLM-referred traffic converts at 30–40%."* ([VentureBeat, 2026](https://venturebeat.com/technology/llm-referred-traffic-converts-at-30-40-and-most-enterprises-arent-optimizing)) and **"58% of marketers say AI-tool visitors convert at higher rates than traditional organic."** ([HubSpot State of Marketing 2026](https://blog.hubspot.com/marketing/answer-engine-optimization-case-studies))
- **"Prompt coverage"** — appearing across the prompts their buyers actually run.
- **"Brand mention rate"** — even unlinked mentions count.
- **"Citation rate"** — how often AI names them when a relevant query is asked.

### The aspirational / emotional dreams

These are the phrases that show up in landing-page testimonials and agency pitches:

- ***"Become the brand AI recommends."***
- ***"Be the answer, not the link on page two."***
- ***"Zero-click dominance"*** (specifically: *"you are paying them to engineer an implied endorsement from the most widely used technologies on earth."* ([SEM Nexus, 2026](https://semnexus.com/is-aeo-worth-it-the-true-roi-of-answer-engine-optimization-in-2026/)))
- ***"Customers arriving pre-sold"*** — backed by: *"An increasing share of SaaS buyers arrive having already been 'recommended' a tool by AI and are not comparison shopping, but validating a decision that AI already helped them make."*
- ***"Future-proof our pipeline."***
- ***"Own the category in AI answers."***

### Concrete result-language from case studies they'll have seen

Copy this tone in your own proof section:

- **"10% of organic traffic now coming from generative engines, 27% of that converting to SQLs"** — Broworks/HubSpot case study ([HubSpot](https://blog.hubspot.com/marketing/answer-engine-optimization-case-studies))
- **"3× better lead conversion from AEO than other sources"** — HubSpot
- **"920% average AI traffic growth"** — AEO Engine
- **"3× more leads, 25× higher conversion rates, 287–415% ROI in 90–120 days"** — widely cited industry figure

> **Note:** the big numbers are widely used but lightly sourced across the industry. If DomiSearch has its own data, use it. Borrowed numbers hurt credibility with Segment B.

## 4. The biggest questions they ask (and expect the page to answer)

Answer these explicitly on the page — ideally with an FAQ section styled to *look* like an AI answer (structure + schema that models reward). Double duty: it'll perform in AEO itself.

1. **"What actually is AEO / GEO / LLMO — and is it the same thing?"**
2. **"Is this just SEO with a new name?"**
3. **"How do you actually measure success?"**
4. **"How long before I see results?"** (honest answer the audience expects: 60–90 days for initial citation movement, 3–6 months for meaningful share of voice)
5. **"Which platforms do you cover — ChatGPT only? Perplexity? AI Overviews? Gemini?"**
6. **"What do you actually do month-to-month?"** (biggest red flag for them: vague retainers)
7. **"How much does it cost?"** (industry baseline they've seen: $2.5k–$7.5k/mo for serious work; $500–$2k for "SEO-mill rebadged")
8. **"Can I do this myself with prompts and schema markup?"**
9. **"What case studies do you have in my industry / ICP?"**
10. **"How do you keep up when the models change weekly?"**
11. **"Will I still need SEO?"**
12. **"What prompts do you track for me, and how?"**

> **Copy implication:** the FAQ isn't filler. For this audience it's a trust-building centrepiece and a working AEO asset. Use proper schema markup.

## 5. Objections you must pre-empt

These will be in the reader's head before they scroll. Address them in copy.

| Objection | Language they use | Pre-empt with |
|-----------|-------------------|---------------|
| "Isn't this just SEO?" | *"AEO is just SEO with extra steps."* / *"Rebrand."* | A clear "here's the 80% overlap with SEO and the 20% that's genuinely different" block. Honesty disarms them. |
| "There's no way to measure this." | *"Vanity metrics."* / *"No GSC for ChatGPT."* | Show your measurement stack up front — which tools, which prompts, which dashboards. |
| "It's too early / too volatile." | *"The models change every week."* | Acknowledge it. Frame your methodology as *resilient to model change* (entity clarity, authority signals, structured content). |
| "My agency already does SEO." | | Offer a layered approach / white-label option. Don't force them to fire anyone. |
| "This is overpriced." | *"Premium for rebadged work."* | Transparent pricing or pricing-range. Say what's in the retainer. |
| "I can do this myself." | *"I'll just add FAQ schema."* | Respect it. Acknowledge what they can DIY (schema, FAQ formatting) and clearly show what they can't (entity graph work, cross-platform prompt tracking, citation ops, authority pipeline). |
| "Will the results survive the next model change?" | | Methodology section that emphasizes fundamentals (authority, clarity, structured answers) that work across models. |
| "Show me revenue, not citations." | *"Citation share of voice that rings hollow."* — Robert Rose | Always tie citation metrics to downstream funnel. Never end a case study at "we got more citations." |

## 6. Voice & tone playbook

### Overall register

**Confident but non-hypey.** The #1 complaint about AEO marketing is the hype cycle ([Robert Rose, CMI](https://contentmarketinginstitute.com/seo-for-content/answer-engine-optimization)). The safest positioning is *"the calm, technical, results-honest AEO specialist in a market full of shouters."*

Aim for the register of: Aleyda Solís' LinkedIn posts, Kevin Indig's Growth Memo, Ahrefs' blog, Rand Fishkin at SparkToro. Plain-speaking, technically literate, allergic to empty buzzwords, willing to be wrong in public.

Do **not** write like: a SaaS ad in an airport, a Medium post titled "10 Game-Changing Ways AI Will Transform Your Marketing Forever", or a typical AEO-agency homepage circa 2025.

### Sentence-level guidance

- **Short > long.** This audience reads on mobile between meetings.
- **Concrete > abstract.** Say "we track 120 prompts across ChatGPT, Perplexity and Google AI Overviews," not "comprehensive AI visibility coverage."
- **Numbers over adjectives.** "Average 47% citation share for our SaaS clients" beats "industry-leading."
- **Name the thing.** Use LLM names (ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, Claude, Google AI Overviews). Specificity reads as expertise.
- **Don't mystify.** The audience is tired of AEO being framed as mysterious. Demystifying is itself a selling point.

### Two phrases the page should probably NOT use

- **"Game-changer"**, **"revolutionary"**, **"unlock"**, **"elevate"** — the hype words Robert Rose and his audience actively mock.
- **"AI-first"** as a standalone adjective — empty signal.

### Insider signals (use sparingly, to establish credibility with Segment B/D)

Dropping 2–3 of these across the page signals to SEO pros you know what you're talking about — without alienating Segment A:

- "Entity clarity" / "entity graph"
- "Retrieval layer"
- "Prompt coverage" / "prompt occupancy"
- "Share of voice in LLMs"
- "Citation rate" / "brand mention rate"
- "Model bias" (different LLMs favor different source types — ChatGPT loves Wikipedia at 47.9% of top citations, Perplexity loves Reddit at 46.7%)
- "Zero-click dominance"

### Vocabulary cheat sheet

**Service labels the audience actually uses (in rough order of familiarity):**
AEO (Answer Engine Optimization) · GEO (Generative Engine Optimization) · AI SEO · LLM SEO · LLMO · AI search optimization · ChatGPT SEO · AI visibility

> **Rec:** Lead with "AEO (Answer Engine Optimization)" as the H1 but explicitly cover the synonyms on the page — both for search and because different segments use different terms. Include a one-liner like *"Also known as GEO, LLMO, or AI SEO — it's all the same job."*

**Platform names to mention (they ARE the keywords):**
ChatGPT · Perplexity · Google AI Overviews (AIO) · Gemini · Claude · Copilot · Meta AI

**Phrases that signal you get it (steal liberally):**
- "Show up when your buyers ask ChatGPT"
- "Get cited, not just crawled"
- "Be the answer, not the blue link"
- "Your buyers are asking AI first — make sure the answer mentions you"
- "Prompt-level visibility"
- "Citation share of voice"
- "The SEO rules still matter. We layer on what's new."

**Phrases to avoid (flagged as cringe by the audience):**
- "Unlock the power of AI search"
- "Revolutionary AI visibility platform"
- "Dominate AI search overnight"
- "Be #1 in ChatGPT" (no #1 exists — shows you don't understand the space)

## 7. Concrete copy direction — what the AEO page should do

### Hero section

- **Headline angle options (in order of my preference):**
  1. *Plain-speak, pain-forward:* "When your buyers ask ChatGPT, does the answer mention you?"
  2. *Anti-hype / insider:* "AEO without the buzzwords. Get cited across ChatGPT, Perplexity and Google AI Overviews."
  3. *Outcome-first:* "Become the brand AI recommends in your category."
- **Sub-head:** a single sentence stating what you do + what proof the reader will see below. e.g. *"We engineer your brand into the answers AI gives — tracked across [X] prompts, on ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini and Google AI Overviews. Here's how."*
- **CTA:** resist "Book a strategy call." Try something more specific — *"See a free prompt audit for your brand"* or *"See where you rank in ChatGPT today."*

### Page structure that would resonate

1. **Hero** — pain-forward headline, specific platforms, one CTA.
2. **The "yes, some of this is hype" block.** Short, direct, disarming. Stolen energy: *"Some AEO is rebadged SEO. Here's what's actually different about what we do."* Immediate trust builder.
3. **How buyers actually search now.** 3–4 stats (HubSpot's crash, 89% of B2B buyers using AI, 47% prefer ChatGPT, LLM traffic converts 30–40%). Short, citable, punchy.
4. **What we actually do.** Bullet the services concretely — prompt research, entity work, structured answers, citation ops across platforms, authority pipeline (PR, Reddit, third-party listings), measurement. *Name the work, don't abstract it.*
5. **How we measure it.** Show the dashboard, name the tools, list the metrics. This is the #1 thing that separates you from SEO mills.
6. **Case study / proof.** One detailed walkthrough > five logos. Include prompt screenshots of before/after where possible — *that* is the format this audience trusts.
7. **"What's in the retainer."** Transparent pricing or at least a pricing range, and what each tier includes. Critical for Segment A.
8. **FAQ (schema-marked, AEO-optimized itself).** Use the 12 questions from §4. Meta-message: *"This FAQ is designed to be cited by AI — this is literally what we do for clients."*
9. **Who this is for / who this isn't for.** Counter-intuitively disqualifying builds trust with all four segments.
10. **CTA + audit offer.**

### Proof assets to build (if budget allows)

In rough priority:

- **Live prompt dashboard / public demo** — pick a category and show, live, how AEO work moves a brand's citation rate. This is what Segment B will screenshot into Slack.
- **Before/after screenshots** of ChatGPT and Perplexity answers for a real client.
- **One-pager: "What to tell your CEO about AEO"** — built specifically for Segment B/C's internal-buy-in pain (§2.5). Gated or free download.
- **Case study with prompt-level data** — not "we got more traffic" but "we went from being named in 2 of 40 category prompts to 27 of 40 in 90 days."

### What *not* to put on the page

- Stock photos of a glowing brain or a robot hand touching a human hand.
- The word "synergy."
- Generic testimonials like "they were great to work with."
- A claim you can guarantee #1 in ChatGPT — instant credibility loss with Segments B/D.
- Vague retainer descriptions ("AI visibility optimization across all major platforms"). Name the work.

## 8. Three testable hero headlines

Ship these as A/B variants rather than picking one in the dark:

1. **"When your buyers ask ChatGPT, is the answer your competitor?"** — pain-forward, specific, punchy.
2. **"AEO without the buzzwords. Just the work that gets you cited."** — anti-hype positioning, appeals to Segment B's skepticism.
3. **"Become the brand AI recommends."** — aspirational, Segment C/A-friendly, most "brandable."
