# Aesthetics industry - pain points & AEO angle

Research brief for the DomiSearch Meta-ads campaign targeting UK non-surgical
aesthetic clinics (injectables, skin, laser). Compiled June 2026.

> Purpose: ground the landing-page copy in the real language, fears and
> commercial pressures of clinic owners so the page speaks their world, not ours.

---

## 1. The market they operate in

- UK aesthetics sector valued at **£3.6bn**, growing **>10% a year**, projected
  **£5.1bn by 2028**.
- Non-surgical treatments **surged 23% in 2024**.
- **~19,701 registered practitioners**, **~5,500 Botox clinics**, **~900,000
  Botox treatments/year**.
- Injectables ≈ **65% of industry revenue**.
- Licensing reforms are pulling more qualified nurses, pharmacists and doctors
  in - so competition is *rising*, not levelling off.

**What it means:** a crowded, fast-growing market where "every clinic looks the
same" to a prospective patient. Differentiation and visibility are the whole game.

## 2. The pain points (in their language)

**a) The treatments they're known for can't be advertised.**
Botox (and brand variants Vistabel/Dysport) is a **prescription-only medicine
(POM)**. Under CAP rule 12.12 and MHRA rules it **cannot be advertised to the
public** - no paid ads, and clinics can't even name it on their own social
channels (hashtags count as implied ads). The **MHRA issued 47 enforcement
notices in 2024**, mostly on Instagram/TikTok, and ASA/MHRA now use AI to detect
violations. Clinics may reference it on their website only in tightly limited,
post-consultation contexts.
→ *The single biggest lever - their flagship treatment is one they legally can't
buy their way to visibility for. Organic / AI-search recommendation is one of the
few compliant routes left.*

**b) Patients don't pick the nearest clinic - they pick the most trusted one.**
This is a high-consideration, fear-driven purchase (fear of bad results, "fake"
look, complications, regret). Before booking, patients: search "best Botox clinic
near me" / "lip filler [city]", read and cross-check Google reviews, study
before-and-afters, ask in Facebook groups, watch YouTube - and increasingly ask
**ChatGPT / Google AI** "which clinic should I choose" and "is X clinic
reputable". Trust, credentials and reputation beat proximity and price.

**c) New clinics are invisible on Google for ~a year.**
Only **1.74% of new webpages** reach Google's top 10 within their first year; the
average #1-ranking page is ~5 years old. Combined with **£45+ per cosmetic Google
Ads click**, the paid + organic Google route is slow and expensive.

**d) Discovery has moved into AI answers.**
Health is now one of the most common uses of ChatGPT; OpenAI reports ~40M daily
US health queries and launched **ChatGPT Health in Jan 2026**. When AI names a
shortlist of clinics, the un-named clinics simply don't exist at the decision
point - and (unlike Google Ads) you can't pay to appear there.

**e) Speed-to-lead and the "tyre-kicker" problem.**
Patients who get a response within 5 minutes book at ~5x the rate; most clinics
take hours. Clinics also burn time on low-intent enquiries and no-shows.

## 3. The DomiSearch angle

> "When someone asks AI for the best clinic near them, does it name you - or your
> competitor? You can't pay your way into that answer, and Botox rules mean you
> can't advertise your way there either. AI visibility is the new word-of-mouth."

This reframes AEO from a vague tech service into the **compliant growth channel**
for a market that has been squeezed out of conventional advertising for its core
treatments. It speaks to fear (invisibility, competitor wins), to compliance
(safe by design), and to trust (being *recommended*, not advertised).

## 4. Copy / language bank

Words and phrases that sound like their world (use; don't sound like an agency):
clinic, practitioner, injector, consultation, patient/client, fully booked,
before-and-afters, natural results, reputable, qualified, complications,
compliant, ASA/MHRA, deposit, no-shows, "best clinic near me", word-of-mouth.

Avoid: "leads", "funnels", "AEO/GEO" jargon up front, generic agency speak.

## 5. Stat caveats before going public

- "40M daily health queries" / ChatGPT Health figures are **US / OpenAI-reported**
  - fine as context, do **not** present as UK clinic-specific.
- Use UK market stats (£3.6bn, 5,500 clinics, 23%) as the defensible public
  numbers. Anything clinic-specific on the page should trace to a real DomiSearch
  result before we publish it (same discipline as the Taxd stat).
